Number Please

Privacy policy

Effective November 20, 2026

Number Please is a private telephone exchange run by one household for its own use, at numberplease.app. This policy explains what information it handles, why, and who else sees it. Questions: martin.mccafferty@gmail.com.

What is collected

  • Your Google account email, when you sign in with Google. It is used only to check that you are one of the household's accounts and to keep you signed in (a signed cookie, for up to 30 days). Nothing else from your Google profile is requested.
  • Google Home access, if a household owner connects it. Google gives the exchange an access grant for the household's Google Home, which is kept on the exchange's server and never sent to a browser.
  • What is said on calls. Speech on the phone is sent to speech and AI services (below) so the operator can understand and answer. Calls are not recorded or kept; text of what was said may appear in the server's short-term operating logs, used only to fix problems.
  • Things you ask it to keep: the shopping list, reminders, routines and the exchange's settings (such as the home location for the weather), stored on the exchange's server.

How Google user data is used

Google Home data (the household's homes, rooms and devices, their state, and the actions a caller asks for) is used only to answer the household's own requests: to tell a caller whether a light is on, to change a thermostat setting, or to run an action the caller asked for. It is read again every few minutes so device names stay up to date. It is not used for advertising, not sold, not used to build profiles, and not shared with anyone except as needed to carry out the caller's request (for example, the device names the operator needs are passed to the AI service that holds the conversation).

Number Please's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Who else handles it

  • Anthropic (Claude): the conversation with the operator.
  • OpenAI: turning speech into text, and some of the voices.
  • Google: sign-in, Google Home, and some of the voices (Gemini).
  • SwitchBot: the household's SwitchBot devices, if connected.
  • Open-Meteo: the weather, given only the home location. News comes from public NPR and BBC feeds.
  • Railway: hosts the server where the information above is stored.

Each handles data under its own terms. Nothing is sold or shared with anyone else.

Removing it

A household owner can disconnect Google Home at any time on the settings page, which deletes the stored grant; you can also remove Number Please's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions. To have your email or any other information removed, write to the address above.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date.
